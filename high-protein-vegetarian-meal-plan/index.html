High-protein vegetarian meal plan

One 43g breakfast. One 39g lunch. Repeat when life is busy.

This egg-free plan uses dairy and plant protein to put roughly 82 grams of protein on autopilot before dinner. Calculate your products once, save the number and stop rebuilding your day in a tracking app.

Breakfast · about 8 minutes

Greek-yogurt chocolate smoothie + avocado toast

≈ 43g≈ 490 calories

Make the smoothie

  1. 1

    Add one serving chocolate protein powder that provides about 21g protein.

  2. 2

    Add ½ cup plain nonfat Greek yogurt, ¼ cup milk, ¼ banana and a handful (about ½ cup) frozen blueberries.

  3. 3

    Add ¾ cup water and ice, then blend until smooth. Add a little more water if it is too thick.

Make the toast

Toast one regular slice Dave’s 21 Whole Grains and Seeds and top with ¼ avocado, salt, black pepper and red pepper flakes.

Can I add Greek yogurt to a protein smoothie?

Yes—it is the easiest way to make this breakfast reach 40g.

Greek yogurt makes the smoothie thicker and adds about 12g protein per half cup in this example. With 21g powder, milk and fruit, the smoothie contributes about 36g; the avocado toast takes the complete breakfast to roughly 43g.

Make the toast

Toast one regular slice Dave’s 21 Whole Grains and Seeds. Spoon ½ cup cottage cheese over it, add ¼ sliced or smashed avocado, then season with pepper and red pepper flakes.

Make the chaas

Whisk or blend ¾ cup plain nonfat Greek yogurt with cold water until drinkable. Season with salt, mint powder, black pepper and any spices you like.

Cottage cheese and seasoned chaas can both contain substantial sodium. Taste before adding salt and choose lower-sodium products if that is appropriate for you.

How the example was calculated

Use these numbers as a starting point—not a prescription.

The branded example uses current manufacturer nutrition facts: Orgain plant protein powder provides 21g protein and 150 calories per serving; Dave’s regular 21 Whole Grains and Seeds provides 6g and 110 calories per slice; FAGE Total 0% provides 18g and 90 calories per ¾ cup; and Good Culture 2% cottage cheese provides 14g and 90 calories per ½ cup. Fruit, milk and avocado are rounded food-database estimates.

Sources: Orgain nutrition, Dave’s Killer Bread nutrition, FAGE Total 0% nutrition, Good Culture nutrition, Vega protein FAQ and USDA FoodData Central.

Common questions

Keep the numbers useful—not rigid.

Is this meal plan vegetarian and egg-free?

Yes. It contains dairy but no eggs or meat. It is lacto-vegetarian, not vegan. If you eat eggs, the original egg-based breakfast and lunch is another repeatable option.

Can I use flavored Greek yogurt?

You can, but protein and added sugar vary. Plain strained Greek yogurt makes the label math easier and works in both the sweet smoothie and savory chaas.

Can I replace Greek yogurt with vegan yogurt?

Yes, but many plant-based yogurts have much less protein. Check the label and increase another protein source if you want to preserve the meal total.

Do I need exactly 100 grams of protein?

No. One hundred grams is an example, not a universal target. Your needs depend on body size, activity, age, health and goals. The repeatable-meal system also works with a lower or higher appropriate target.

Do I have to eat these meals every day?

No. They are defaults for busy days. Rotate when you are bored, eat something else when you go out and keep variety across dinners and the week.

A note about nutrition: This is a practical food example, not individualized medical or dietetic advice, and it does not promise weight loss. Calories and protein vary by brand and portion. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have kidney disease, diabetes, food allergies, another medical condition or a history of disordered eating, discuss major dietary changes and protein targets with an appropriately qualified professional.
