My repeatable protein method

How I aim for 100g of protein without tracking every meal.

I calculate the breakfast and lunch I repeat, save those numbers, and then eat the same dinner I make for my children. Fewer variables make the routine easier to continue when work, home and family are already asking for my attention.

By Priyanka · Personal experience and calculated examples · September 2, 2026

The short answer

Calculate two meals once. Reuse the answer.

I do not try to construct a brand-new 100g day every morning. My breakfast and lunch provide roughly 75–80g using my usual products and portions. My afternoon Costco edamame adds about 9–11g, leaving only a modest amount for the family dinner.

My repeatable breakfast

Seasoned egg whites + chocolate protein smoothie

≈ 39–40g≈ 350 calories

What I use

  • 4 boiled egg whites, topped with salt, black pepper, red pepper flakes and everything-bagel seasoning
  • 1 serving (2 scoops) Orgain Chocolate Fudge protein powder
  • ¼ cup whole milk and ¾ cup water
  • ¼ banana and a handful of frozen blueberries
  • 4 almonds

I have repeated this breakfast for months because I genuinely enjoy it. Repetition works better when the default tastes good—not when it feels like punishment.

Make the toast

Fry two eggs and top them with salt and red pepper flakes. Toast one 110-calorie slice of Dave’s Killer Bread, spread with ¼–½ avocado, and add the eggs.

Make the chaas

Thin one label serving of Kirkland plain Greek yogurt with cold water. Season it with salt, mint powder, black pepper and any spices you enjoy.

*Cooking oil or butter is not included because the amount used varies. It adds calories but essentially no protein. Your yogurt and bread labels take priority over these estimates.

Dinner comes from the family pot

I eat what I make for my children.

I do not want a separate “diet dinner.” After breakfast, lunch and edamame, even a lighter family meal may be enough to close the remaining gap.

Usually closes the gap

Dal, chole or rajma with rice

Often ≈ 18–25g

A substantial serving of lentils or beans plus rice usually clears the remaining gap comfortably.

Check the main ingredient

Two roti + sabzi

Often ≈ 8–18g

Paneer, soy, peas or bean-based sabzi can be higher. A mostly vegetable sabzi may need dal, Greek yogurt or another protein side.

Easy to strengthen

Pasta with vegetables

Often ≈ 10–18g

The pasta portion and brand matter. Beans, peas, cheese, tofu, lentil pasta or chickpea pasta can turn it into a more reliable 20g dinner.

Dinner values are broad estimates because family recipes and serving sizes vary significantly. Check each common family dinner once; you do not need to log it every night.

The no-daily-tracking workflow

Do the thinking before the busy moment.

This is not “never look at a label.” It is using labels once so you do not have to reopen an app at every meal.

  1. 01

    Choose defaults

    Pick one breakfast and one lunch that you genuinely like enough to repeat.

  2. 02

    Calculate once

    Use the packages and portions in your own kitchen. Save the totals in a note.

  3. 03

    Map family dinners

    Learn which three or four regular dinners close your remaining gap and which need a side.

  4. 04

    Repeat or rotate

    Repeat for several days. Change the default when you want—not because the plan demands novelty.

Common questions

Keep the structure. Leave room for life.

Can I get 100g of protein without tracking macros every day?

You can reduce daily tracking by calculating repeatable meals once and keeping a small set of known dinner patterns. This is less precise than logging every ingredient, but it can provide useful structure without requiring constant data entry.

Does everyone need 100g of protein?

No. One hundred grams is my personal example, not a universal recommendation. Needs differ with body size, age, activity, goals, pregnancy or breastfeeding status and health.

What if my dinner is low in protein?

Nothing has failed. The edamame snack reduces how much dinner needs to supply. You can accept a lower day or add a familiar side such as Greek yogurt, dal, beans, paneer or tofu. The point is consistency over time, not forcing every day to equal exactly 100.

Do I have to eat the same meals forever?

No. Repeat for a week, rotate between two known options, or replace a meal when you are ready. Calculate the new option once and it becomes another usable default.

Can I use an egg-free version?

Yes. The high-protein vegetarian meal plan includes egg-free breakfasts and lunches with calculated examples.
