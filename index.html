The real problem

Meal tracking asks for attention at exactly the moment life has used it up.

Work, children, home and appointments—by dinner, feeding yourself can feel like one decision too many. A new recipe means new ingredients, portions and app entries. Miss a few days and the tracking habit disappears.

My answer was not a more detailed plan. It was a smaller one: make breakfast and lunch predictable, then let dinner stay human.

What I actually eat

Breakfast and lunch, on repeat.

These are defaults, not commandments. Season them your way, rotate when you want and use the labels on the products you actually buy.

Breakfast

Egg whites + chocolate smoothie

≈ 38–48g*
  • Seasoned egg whites: 4 boiled egg whites topped with salt, black pepper, red pepper flakes and everything-bagel seasoning
  • Chocolate protein smoothie: ¼ banana, a handful of frozen blueberries, ¼ cup milk, ¾ cup water and 1 serving Vega or Orgain chocolate protein powder
  • 4 almonds

About 8 minutes once the egg whites are ready

Lunch

Egg-and-avocado toast + chaas

≈ 38–44g*
  • Seasoned fried eggs: 2 fried eggs topped with salt and red pepper flakes
  • Avocado toast: 2 slices Dave’s Killer Bread topped with ¼–½ smashed avocado
  • Greek-yogurt chaas: 1 full serving plain Greek yogurt thinned with water, then seasoned with salt, mint powder, black pepper and spices of your choice

About 10 minutes

*The powder, milk, bread and Greek yogurt create the biggest variation. My usual combination lands around 80–85g across both meals; yours may differ.

Common questions

Keep the method. Lose the rigidity.

Do I need exactly 100 grams of protein?

No. One hundred grams is a memorable example, not a universal prescription. Your appropriate target depends on body size, activity, age, health and goals. The repeatable-meal method still works if your target is 70, 80 or 120 grams.

Is eating the same breakfast and lunch every day healthy?

Repeating two meals can be compatible with a varied diet when dinner, produce and occasional rotations provide broader nutrients. The goal is a useful default—not a rule you can never break.

Why is the protein estimate a range?

Protein powders, breads, milk and Greek yogurts vary by product and serving size. Check those four labels once, write down your total and reuse that number whenever you repeat the meal.

What if I get bored?

Create two default breakfasts and two default lunches. Rotate weekly or whenever you want a change. Fewer choices is the benefit; zero choice is not the goal.

A note about your target: 100g is the author’s personal example, not a recommendation for every woman. Protein and calorie needs differ. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have kidney disease, another medical condition, or a history of disordered eating, discuss major dietary changes with an appropriately qualified healthcare professional.
