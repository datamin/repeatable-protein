My postpartum reset—twice

The simple routine that helped me lose 30+ pounds—twice. Without making life more complicated.

I am a full-time working mom with two kids. After each pregnancy, I took things slowly and gave myself time. When I felt ready to return to a routine, I realized I could not manage endless meal choices, complicated tracking and constantly changing workouts.

I wanted to eat well and build strength, but I needed both to be simple enough for real life. Two repeatable meals and three familiar workouts are what helped me.

The real problem

Meal tracking asks for attention at exactly the moment motherhood has used it up.

Work, two children, home and appointments—by dinner, feeding myself could feel like one decision too many. A new recipe meant new ingredients, portions and app entries. When there were too many variables, the tracking habit disappeared.

My answer was not a more detailed plan. It was a smaller one: make breakfast and lunch predictable, repeat workouts I already understood, then let dinner and the rest of life stay human.

Seven months and still repeating

Repeating a meal is not boring when the meal is genuinely delicious.

I have eaten this egg-white breakfast for seven months because I still enjoy it. The fried-egg avocado toast at lunch is something I look forward to as well. A repeatable default should not feel like punishment—it should be food that tastes good enough to choose again while making one part of the day easier.

My going-out rule

Eat the protein breakfast before stepping out.

On restaurant days, travel days or busy family days, I may not reach my usual 80–85 grams by lunch—and that is okay. Having roughly 40 grams at breakfast gives the day a strong start. Then I can go out, enjoy the experience and make the best choices available without turning one flexible meal into an all-or-nothing day.

Why repetition matters

“Things can look the same every day—until one day everything changes.”

One less decision at breakfast. One less decision at lunch. One workout you already know. Each simple repeat puts you one step closer to the person you are trying to become.

Three days · about 30–35 minutes each

Same exercises. One small improvement at a time.

Why strength matters to me

“I want to be strong enough for my life—not only smaller on a scale.”

As a woman and a mother, building strength is part of taking care of myself. I want the energy and physical confidence to be present for my children, my family and everything life asks of me. Caring for everyone else should not mean disappearing from my own priorities.

The progression rule

Earn the next rep. Then earn the next weight.

  1. Repeat the plan. Keep the same exercises long enough to learn them.
  2. Add one good rep. Progress only when your range of motion and form stay controlled.
  3. Then add a little weight. Use the smallest available increase and build the reps again.

The weights above are my recorded working weights, not recommendations for everyone. The screenshots did not show reps or weights for four Pull movements, so those are intentionally listed as three sets only. Use a load appropriate for your experience and stop if an exercise causes pain.

For context, federal guidance recommends muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days each week and increasing it gradually; this personal three-day routine is one way I make that repeatable. Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans

Common questions

Keep the method. Lose the rigidity.

Do I need exactly 100 grams of protein?

No. One hundred grams is a memorable example, not a universal prescription. Your appropriate target depends on body size, activity, age, health and goals. The repeatable-meal method still works if your target is 70, 80 or 120 grams.

Is eating the same breakfast and lunch every day healthy?

Repeating two meals can be compatible with a varied diet when dinner, produce and occasional rotations provide broader nutrients. The goal is a useful default—not a rule you can never break.

Why is the protein estimate a range?

Protein powders, breads, milk and Greek yogurts vary by product and serving size. Check those four labels once, write down your total and reuse that number whenever you repeat the meal.

What if I get bored?

Create two default breakfasts and two default lunches. Rotate weekly or whenever you want a change. Fewer choices is the benefit; zero choice is not the goal.

A note about your target, training and postpartum recovery: This page describes one woman’s experience, not a promise of weight loss or an individualized program. 100g and the displayed workout weights are personal examples. Recovery after childbirth differs; resume exercise gradually when it is medically safe for you, particularly after a cesarean birth or complications. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, have kidney disease, another medical condition, an injury, or a history of disordered eating, discuss major dietary or exercise changes with an appropriately qualified professional.
