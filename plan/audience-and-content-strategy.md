# Audience and content strategy

Last updated: 2026-09-02

## Audience order

1. Indian women in the United States and India who are balancing work, home, children, and their own health.
2. Busy women in the United States who want a simpler high-protein routine.

The audience focus does not change the core method: choose a breakfast and lunch that taste good, calculate them once, repeat them during busy weeks, and keep dinner flexible.

## Localization rules

| Content element | India version | US version |
|---|---|---|
| Measurements | Grams plus common household measures | Grams plus cups, tablespoons, ounces when useful |
| Ingredients | Paneer, curd/dahi, chaas, dal, chana, rajma, soy chunks, tofu | Keep Indian foods and add accessible grocery-store substitutes |
| Product data | Prefer the actual Indian package label or ICMR–NIN food data | Prefer the actual US package label or USDA FoodData Central |
| Language | Explain terms once; retain familiar Indian food names | Define terms such as chaas, dahi, or soy chunks without replacing them |
| Planning context | Tiffin, home cooking, vegetarian/eggetarian households | Work lunch, meal prep, school schedules, Indian grocery availability |

## Trust requirements before broader promotion

- Every page identifies Priyanka and labels personal experience clearly.
- Nutrition pages state portions, data sources, uncertainty, and update date.
- Prescriptive postpartum, clinical, or individualized nutrition guidance waits for an appropriately qualified reviewer.
- Any future affiliate relationship, sponsorship, or paid placement is disclosed on the relevant page.
