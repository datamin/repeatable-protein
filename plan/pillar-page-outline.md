# Pillar page outline

## Working title

How to Eat 100g of Protein Without Tracking Every Meal

## Promise

Calculate a repeatable breakfast and lunch once, rotate when useful, and leave dinner flexible.

## Story order

1. Recognize the reader's problem: work, children, home responsibilities, and tracking fatigue.
2. Reveal the full method immediately: a repeatable breakfast and lunch provide roughly 70–85 g; dinner supplies the remainder.
3. Reframe the failure: the system creates too many daily decisions; it is not a lack of willpower.
4. Clarify that 100 g is an example, not a universal target, and show how to determine an appropriate target.
5. Show the founder's actual breakfast and lunch with quantities, protein, calories, fiber, and preparation time.
6. Provide a small number of vegetarian, eggetarian, and dairy-free rotations.
7. Teach readers to construct their own repeatable combination.
8. Explain how to adjust calories without returning to daily tracking.
9. Give flexible dinner templates and protein backup options.
10. Briefly connect the system to a simple progressive strength-training habit.
11. Answer objections and safety questions.
12. Offer a seven-day experiment and a printable or interactive planner.

## Required supporting elements

- US and Indian ingredient variations.
- Metric and US customary quantities.
- Transparent nutrition calculations and sources.
- Evidence citations for protein guidance.
- Appropriate medical and nutritional caveats.
- Author experience plus qualified expert review.
- Original food photographs when available.
- Links to the breakfast, lunch, meal-plan, and planner pages.
